Point 3 · Volvo CE India: operations and offering

Volvo CE India: the Peenya hub

The plant we visited is Volvo CE India's headquarters and factory. It builds the 20–22 tonne excavators that make up most of the Indian market, along with soil compactors and asphalt pavers.

  • WhereIndia HQ and plant, Peenya Industrial Area, Bengaluru
  • HistorySite dates to 1978; Volvo CE in India since 1997. A SEK 144 m (≈ ₹90 crore) upgrade in 2011 added a new excavator line, planned to triple capacity. The first EC210B Prime and EC290B rolled off it that September.
  • BuildsCrawler excavators, soil compactors and asphalt pavers. The 21 t EC210 is about 70% localised, and Peenya exports to similar emerging markets.
  • Network19 dealers, 800+ dealer staff and 180+ touchpoints
  • EngineeringBengaluru Technology Centre: R&D and localisation for India and global markets

    Point 4 · Group 3 problem statement

    Can the machine shop make two more a shift?

    The production manager has to lift output by 20%, from 10 to 12 excavators in one 8-hour shift, without hurting quality. We read the brief as 10 today and 12 as the target, because 20% of 10 is exactly two machines.

    Takt falls by 17%, so the slowest operation needs about 20% more capacity.

    Ground rules we set ourselves

    • Same 8-hour shift and the same headcount
    • No compromise on quality (first-pass yield, Cpk) or safety
    • Fix the process before buying capacity
    • Scope: the machine shop feeding the EC210 / EC215 / EC220 line

    Point 4 · The six levers

    Six levers, one production system

    We anchored every lever in the Volvo Production System: teamwork at the base, process stability, built-in quality and just-in-time as pillars, and continuous improvement on top. Theory of Constraints tells us where to start: at OP30, because a minute saved at the bottleneck is a minute of plant output.

    Point 4 · Quality

    Faster without cutting corners

    The brief says the extra output must not affect quality. Our plan protects it by design.

    • Waste, not speed75 of the 85 freed minutes come from removing losses. The only speed gain (30 → 29 min) comes from validated programs and tooling, not operators rushing.
    • No inspection step removedCMM checks stay, and in-machine probing adds a 100% check of critical bores before unloading.
    • Controlled changeEvery program or tooling change is trialled on five frames, measured and approved before release.
    • Less firefightingStable machines and steady flow mean fewer rushed recoveries, the most common hidden cause of defects.

    Stop-and-fix triggers (jidoka)

    If either line is crossed, the change stops and a root-cause analysis starts.

    FPY < 98%first-pass yield at OP30 (target 98.4%)
    Cpk < 1.33on any critical pin bore

    First-pass yield rises from 95.2% to 98.4%, so fewer faulty frames reach the paint shop and the assembly line.

    Point 4 · Implementation

    Twelve weeks to twelve a shift

    A Plan–Do–Check–Act roadmap. Operators are involved from day one through kaizen workshops, and the gains are not used to cut headcount. The plant manager sponsors it; the production manager owns the KPI board.

    1. Weeks 1–2 · PlanDiagnose
      • Gemba walks and time studies
      • OEE baseline at OP30
      • Cross-functional kaizen team
      Output 10.0 / shift
    2. Weeks 3–6 · DoQuick wins
      • 5S and visual boards
      • SMED workshop at OP30
      • FIFO lane, kits, jib crane
      Output ≈11.0 / shift
    3. Weeks 7–10 · DoStructural fixes
      • TPM steps 1–3
      • Probing, quick clamps, CNC paths
      • Poka-yoke; move drilling to OP50
      Output ≈11.6 / shift
    4. Weeks 11–12 · Check & ActLock it in
      • Verify FPY and Cpk
      • Freeze standard work
      • Roll out to OP10, OP20, OP40
      Output 12.0 / shift

    Risks and how we handle them

    RiskMitigation
    Gains are not sustainedStandard work, layered process audits, daily tier meetings, KPI board
    Faster cycles hurt qualityValidated parameters, capability studies, jidoka stop-and-fix triggers
    Operator resistanceInvolvement in kaizen, training and skill matrix, recognition, no headcount cuts

    What we learned at Peenya

    Capacity is often already in the plant, hidden in waste.

    OP30 loses 120 of its 420 minutes every shift. Six levers win back 75 of them and one minute of cycle time. That is enough for 12 excavators a shift, with no new machine, no extra shift and better quality. Even if only 70% of the gains hold, the plan still reaches 12.
